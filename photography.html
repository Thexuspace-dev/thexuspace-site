Photography

A practice in composition and perspective that feeds directly back into the design work — started in 2019. Two images have since placed as AGORA Top 10 Finalists. Scroll through — this is the story behind four of them.

Scroll to walk through ↓
Vancouver, 2019
01 — Where it started

Attention is the whole craft

It started in 2019 — a harbor, an early morning, a seal surfacing for air I almost missed. I hadn't studied photography formally; I just knew I wanted to catch what most people walk past. That image ended up an AGORA Top 10 Finalist. It taught me the first lesson before I knew I was learning one: the craft isn't the camera, it's paying attention.

Maine, 2020
02 — The opposite lesson

The strongest frame is often the emptiest

A year later, a lighthouse in Maine — pared down to almost nothing: one structure, one light, one horizon. It became my second AGORA Top 10 Finalist, and it taught me the opposite lesson from the seal. Sometimes the work isn't finding more to include, it's having the nerve to leave everything else out.

Idaho, 2021
03 — What waiting teaches

Photography as a kind of patience

Idaho, 2021 — hours outside in the cold, waiting for the sky to do something. Night photography doesn't reward impatience. You set up, you wait, and mostly nothing happens until, for a few minutes, everything does. It's the closest this practice gets to meditation, and the part I didn't expect to need.

RoamWithPete
04 — Where it feeds back

The same eye, pointed at a room

This became the RoamWithPete cover — an aerial view that's really about the same thing I chase in interior design: how a space reads as a whole before it reads as its details. Photography taught me to see composition first. Design is where I get to build it, on purpose, from the ground up.

"Photography and design turned out to be the same eye, pointed in two directions." — Peter Xu
